SESSION 3 — the look becomes the standard. Motion language + icons + a skill.

WHY
Review feedback: the continuous shot works, but the components were plain boxes
and the motion lacked the polish of ByteByteGo-style explainers. Researched what
they use (After Effects — so a hand-keyframed pipeline, not a rival code tool)
and probed what Manim can actually do before promising anything.

WHAT THE PROBE ESTABLISHED (kept because it stops the question being re-asked)
- Vector icons: yes. SVGMobject imports Lucide cleanly.
- Glow: no native blur, but concentric stroked copies look right.
- Gradients, 55 easing curves, TracedPath comet trails: all present in 0.21.
- Motion blur: not in Manim; an ffmpeg tmix frame-blend pass costs about the
  clip's own duration and is now wired into the render profiles.
- True gaussian blur / soft drop shadow behind a filled card: still not possible
  without post-processing. This is the one real gap.
- Benchmarked at 1080p60: a 12-layer stroke glow renders in ~1.4s per
  animation-second; an equivalent pre-blurred ImageMobject sprite takes ~5.6s.
  This is the OPPOSITE of what I assumed going in — Cairo's alpha compositing is
  the bottleneck, not path count, and layer count barely matters. Layered strokes
  are both the better-looking and the cheaper option.

SESSION 4 — vertical column layout, type system, routing guarantees.

WHY
Review of the previous cut: (1) the motion blur made everything feel like slow
motion and shaky, especially on the pull-back; (2) the end-loop trail cut
straight through the boxes instead of following the drawn rails; (3) the
typography at the wide shot looked wrong; (4) the loop was spread too far.

BUILT
- lib/typography.py — type as a share of FRAME HEIGHT, with the point size
  derived from the shot it is read in. Measured calibration: Manim's cap height
  is linear at 0.010128 world units per point, pinned by a test so a Manim
  upgrade cannot silently rescale the back catalogue. This is the real fix for
  "typography looks odd": the old code had arbitrary x2.6 / x1.8 / x2.2
  multipliers and label sizes ranged from 1.8% to 3.5% of frame height with no
  system behind them.
- lib/routing.py — elbow(), join(), and the clearance predicates. The travel
  path is now BUILT FROM the drawn rails, so it cannot disagree with them, and
  the set asserts its own geometry at construction.
- Station gained header_side ("top"/"left") and marquee_side/role/max-width;
  PipelineBox's title moved outside its frame.
- Motion blur is now OFF on every profile, with the reasoning recorded in
  harness.json next to the setting.
- Layout rebuilt as a single vertical column per the brief: chat on top, server,
  then the model box with tokenize/embed/attend/sample stacked, and the token
  climbing back up the right-hand side.

MEASUREMENTS WORTH KEEPING
- Old horizontal layout: 3.59x pull-back. A compact 2x2 circuit reached 1.98x.
  The vertical column asked for is height-bound at 16:9 and lands at 3.24x.
  That trade was made knowingly and stated to the user with the numbers; it is
  survivable only because type is now shot-relative.
- A tall column leaves the sides of a 16:9 frame empty, so station marquees moved
  to the LEFT of their bays — it fills otherwise-wasted margin and keeps the
  stack short.

WHAT WENT WRONG
- The trail bug was NOT what it looked like. The circuit path was clean; the
  cause was attaching the comet to a Dot *before* placing it on the path.
  TracedPath traces get_center from the frame it is added, so the dot's first
  traced point was wherever it happened to sit (the origin), producing one long
  chord across the whole set that looked exactly like a routing failure. Both
  trails now get positioned first. This is in the skill.
- path_clears walks the true curve and therefore cannot see corner-cutting by a
  trail's per-frame chords. Added chord_violations/assert_trail_clears, which
  models what TracedPath actually draws, and the set asserts it at 30 frames.
- Positioned stations with `st.bay.move_to(...)` then shifted the group, which
  moved the bay INSIDE its own group and detached it from its header and
  marquee. Rendered as garbled overlapping labels. One shift of the whole group
  is correct; there is now a comment at both call sites.
- The self-imposed zoom cap in validate() fired when the box title moved
  outside the frame and grew the set. That is the guard working — the fix was to
  make the set genuinely more compact, not to raise the cap.
- Three of my own new tests were wrong rather than the code: a glow bbox
  assertion (Manim's .width ignores stroke), a share-consistency tolerance
  tighter than rasteriser rounding, and a readability check measured on a

SESSION 5 — the ending: typography and the glow-over-label bug.

WHY
Review: "the last typography that appears is bad and the box getting coloured is
very bad."

WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS
Two separate bugs, both of which present as "the text is grey":

1. `set_opacity` on a glow halo. A halo is twelve COPIES of the bay with fill
   cleared; set_opacity raises fill opacity too, so at full glow they became
   twelve opaque plates sitting over the bay's own contents. Every label inside
   a lit bay was buried. The tell, once I finally rendered a frame with a title
   wide enough to overflow its bay: the part of the text OUTSIDE the bay was
   bright white and the part inside was dark. Fixed by animating
   set_stroke(opacity=...) everywhere a halo is involved.
2. Animating the Station VGroup itself (effects.arrive/settle called
   `station.animate.scale`). A VGroup carries its own rgba, transparent by
   default, and interpolating it drags children's opacity down. Dropped the
   scale pop; the glow alone reads as the node reacting.

I burned a lot of this session chasing (1) through the model rather than the
render: every instrumented check said opacity 1.0 and colour #F2A93B at every
beat, because the model WAS correct — it was compositing. The lesson, now in the
skill: when a label looks wrong, check whether the same text outside the
container renders correctly before suspecting colour or the type system.

ALSO CHANGED
- Dropped the two-labels-per-bay scheme (small close-up title + large revealed
  wide label). They overlapped and needed an opacity swap that fought bug (2).
  One title, sized for SHOT_LABEL=26 — between the close-up and the pull-back —
  reads at both and cannot desync from itself.
- Bay titles are bold: at the pull-back a thin stem antialiases to grey long
  before the glyph gets too small to read. Weight buys contrast size does not.
- PipelineBox title is one step smaller, has no subtitle, and is clamped to the
  box width; the server is positioned above the CAPTION rather than the frame,
  which is what had it sitting on top of the words.
- Bays widened to 9.6 and HEADER_SHARE caps a left header at 58% of the bay, so
  a title sized for a wider shot cannot starve the slot. SHOT_TIGHT raised to
  13.6 to match, and the scene's W_TIGHT with it.
- Glow left at a low resting level (0.18) rather than four bays at full.

Reviewed the accepted cut frame by frame after a report that the ending was not
up to standard. Everything found was real. The video-level write-up is in
videos/chatgpt_message_journey/claude-progress.txt; what landed in /lib:

- lib/components/glyph.py: `Glyph.fade_to_opacity`. Icons are stroked outlines
  with the fill cleared, so a blanket `set_opacity` fills them in and they
  render as solid blobs. Any animation that sets an ABSOLUTE opacity on a group
  containing an icon needs this; FadeIn is safe because it interpolates back to
  the authored state. Second time this repo has been bitten by fill-vs-stroke

  is right, check which channel carries its colour.
- lib/components/chat_ui.py: `ChatWindow.place`/`post` put an externally built
  bubble through the same layout path as `add_message`, and

  scene positioning a bubble by hand near a window is how a reply ended up
  drawn across everything below the window.
- lib/components/factory.py: HEADER_SHARE 0.58 -> 0.36, and `reveal_wide` now
  fades text on fill and icons on stroke. A bay's content is the subject of its

Tests added for all three in tests/test_components_structure.py and

SESSION 7 — three review fixes
================================================================================

Review of the shipped cut: the web-server arrow was not connected, half-boxes
sat in front of the words in the tokenizer and the sampler, and the sampling
bar chart was not aligned to the left. All three were real and all three were
in /lib rather than in the video.

1. THE ARROW ENDED IN SPACE
The rail from the web server stopped above the box CAPTION rather than at the
box, deliberately, so it would not draw through "THE MODEL". But that caption
is a wide-shot label: it is dark through every close-up, so for most of the
film there was an arrow pointing at nothing and a machine that looked
unconnected. The dodge was the bug.

The underlying problem is that a TALL box cannot wear its name across the top.
The rail comes down the column's midline and a title sized to be read at the
pull-back is about as wide as the box, so there is no "shift it aside" that
stays legible — the previous version was clamped to exactly the box width.
PipelineBox gained title_side="side": the name runs down the margin, reading
bottom-to-top. A tall box has height to spare, a column layout leaves its side
margins empty, and the midline comes free. It also balances the return label,
which was already a rotated label in the opposite margin. The rail now ends on
the frame edge.

SHOT_WIDE retuned 57.8 -> 55.0 to match: the box no longer needs headroom.

2. HALF-BOXES IN FRONT OF THE WORDS
The space marker, "␣It". It was drawn on purpose — whitespace belongs to the
token, which is true and worth knowing — but on screen it does not read as a
distinction, it reads as a broken glyph. It needs narration to land and a chip
in a silent 30-second cut has no chance to explain itself. display_token now
trims by default and takes show_space=True for a shot that says out loud what
the marker means. Chips still CARRY the full token; only the drawing changed.

3. RAGGED BARS
label_width capped the label but the track was placed next_to() it, so every
row put its bar wherever that row's word happened to end — "It" and "When"
gave different left edges. label_width now reserves a column and the row is
built against a fixed origin, so labels, tracks and values each share one edge.

WORTH KNOWING
The snapshot gate did not catch (3). A 16x16 luminance signature at tolerance
0.02 cannot see a bar moving a third of a unit. The five affected baselines
were re-approved after looking at the render. Not proposing a change to the
gate — it is protected, and a tighter one would be fragile — but it is a known
blind spot: alignment is a job for a structural assertion, and there are now
two of those.

183 tests (7 new), 20 baselines (5 re-approved), Evaluator green on all five
gates. assets/ refreshed.

Built videos/chatgpt_request_lifecycle: a 45-second continuous shot through the

the four-bay inference stack, then streaming and the after-response fan-out.
Ten stations on four bands in one closed circuit, pulled back to 4.07x at the

This is a NEW video directory, not an edit of chatgpt_message_journey. That
film is committed in assets/ with every feature passing; rewriting it into a
nine-station 45s cut would have destroyed a reviewed deliverable. The two share
the /lib vocabulary, the palette, and the four in-box station accents on
purpose, so the new one reads as the same world seen wider.
videos/chatgpt_message_journey/ is byte-identical to where session 7 left it.

WHAT LANDED IN /lib

- lib/components/checks.py - CheckRow / CheckList. Rows of named checks that
  tick from pending to passed. Four call sites in this film alone (edge,
  gateway, stream, after), which is well past the promotion bar. Two things in
  it are scar tissue rather than taste: the three columns are RESERVED rather
  than measured (session 7's ragged-bar bug, in a new component), and the tick
  is two Lines with no fill at all, so it is shown and hidden on STROKE
  opacity. A blanket set_opacity would draw it as two filled slivers - the same
  fill-vs-stroke trap as the glow halos in session 5 and the Lucide icons in
  session 6. Third time. The docstring says so.

- lib/components/stacked.py - SegmentedBar. A proportional bar cut into named
  segments, with a legend or inline labels. Two load-bearing call sites: the
  orchestrator's prompt breakdown and the KV-cache split.

  min_segment is the whole reason it exists. Seven tokens out of 3,937 is 0.18%
  of the bar: sub-pixel, so the segment does not render, so the beat - "your
  question is the last few tokens of about four thousand" - fails silently with
  the bar looking like it has one fewer part than the legend claims. Each
  segment's DRAWN fraction is floored and the rest renormalised; the values stay
  true and `fractions` reports the unclamped arithmetic. Same trade as
  display_token's show_space, and documented as one: in a silent cut legibility
  beats notation, and the model keeps the real number either way.

- lib/components/probability.py - ONE new keyword argument, value_format. A
  quota meter and a batch-utilisation meter are a ProbabilityBar with a
  different number format, so that is a parameter, not a gauges.py (AGENTS rule
  1). The default is byte-identical to the f-string it replaced; the snapshot
  gate was run before and after and all 20 existing baselines pass untouched.
  Also documented the real hazard on set_value: it REPLACES value_mob, so the
  submobject list changes. It was probed on a throwaway scene before being
  relied on - .animate.set_value does interpolate (Manim aligns the two glyph
  trees) and the number morphs rather than ticking, which over 0.9s reads as a
  meter filling. The documented fallback was not needed.

Tests: 26 new structural tests in tests/test_components_structure.py (97 in
that file, 212 in the suite) and 3 new snapshot cases - check_list,
segmented_bar_legend, segmented_bar_inline - each rendered to a PNG and LOOKED
AT before approval. 23 baselines now. The legend case was re-shot once: at
scale 1.5 the composition was 14.6 units wide and the values were cut off the
right-hand edge of a 14-unit frame.

WHAT THE FRAME REVIEW CAUGHT THAT THE EVALUATOR COULD NOT

Seven real bugs, all found by rendering at the draft profile and reading a
contact sheet and full-size stills. Two are worth repeating here because they
are /lib behaviour rather than this video's:

1. ChatWindow.post -> commit -> _scroll_into_view -> _hide_scrolled_off calls
   set_opacity(1.0) on every message still in view. That UNDOES the hidden
   state a StreamingBubble is built in, so the finished reply was legible from
   the moment it was posted - ten seconds before it was supposed to arrive -
   and every later reveal(n) was a no-op nobody could see. The new scene calls
   reveal(0) immediately after post. chatgpt_message_journey has the same latent
   problem and was NOT changed: it is shipped and reviewed, and fixing it means
   re-rendering and re-reviewing that film. Worth a session of its own.
2. A chat window sized for one bubble silently eats the other. At CHAT_H=6.4 the
   message area is 3.29 units and question + reply need 3.47, so the transcript
   scrolled and hid the question at exactly the moment the answer landed. That
   is ChatWindow behaving as documented; the fix is to size the window against
   `message_area_height` rather than by eye, which is now measured in a comment
   in lifecycle_set.py.

The other five (a black frame mid-shot where the camera swung across 25 units of
empty world, the chat sliced by the frame edge on the travel shot, a caption and
a meter placed under the same bay on top of each other, a fade-out and an
entrance sharing one slot for 1.2s, and an emphasised segment with nothing
naming it) are in the video's own progress log.

None of the seven was visible to any of the five gates. This is the argument for
the review loop, stated again with fresh evidence.
