WHAT WAS VERIFIED AGAINST tiktoken, AND HOW
  The encodings could not be fetched from `openaipublic.blob.core.windows.net`
  (blocked by the environment's network policy). They came from a third-party
  mirror (`pkoukk/tiktoken-go-loader`) and were then AUTHENTICATED against the
  SHA-256 `expected_hash` values hardcoded in `tiktoken_ext/openai_public.py`:
  both `cl100k_base.tiktoken` (223921b7...865b2a7) and `o200k_base.tiktoken`
  (446a9538...cfb1a2d) matched, so the files are byte-identical to what OpenAI
  serves. Cross-checked: cl100k_base encodes "hello world" to [15339, 1917].
  This pre-flight was done BEFORE this session and handed over in the plan; it
  was not re-run here, because the encoding host is unreachable from here.

THE BRIEF'S NINE-CHIP ROW WAS WRONG, AND WHY B2/B4 WERE RESPECIFIED
  The brief's beats 2 and 4 draw nine chips with `st|raw|berry` among them. In
  the real sentence the word carries a leading space and ` strawberry` is ONE
  vocabulary entry, id 101830; the sentence is SEVEN tokens. The three-piece
  split exists only for the bare word. The brief's own ruling was "change the
  chips, not the claim", and the claim survives in a stronger form: the whole
  word — all three r's — is one opaque integer with no piece boundary near
  them. B2 now lands the leading-space rule and the punchline as one gesture
  (the same ten letters are 101830 with a space and 302·1618·19772 without,
  both on screen together); B4 flips seven integers, not nine.

  Also corrected: the question is 27 characters and contains FOUR r's, not the
  brief's 26 and three. The fourth is the standalone `r` in `r's`. Both the
  pulse and the assertion are scoped to the word, and the counter is labelled
  `r in "strawberry" — 3` rather than a bare 3.

TRAPS WORTH REMEMBERING
  * `scenes/props.py` COLLIDES. A video's `scenes/` goes on sys.path as a flat
    namespace, and `chatgpt_request_lifecycle/scenes/props.py` already owns the
    name; the second one silently resolves to whichever imported first, which
    failed the `unit` gate with an ImportError swallowed into "found []". This
    video's is `tokenizer_props.py`.
  * A prop parked at `set_opacity(0)` cannot be brought in with `FadeIn` — the
    fade targets the opacity it already carries, i.e. nothing. Staged props are
    DETACHED from the set group instead and added by their own beat.
  * The B6 set-shrink is `Transform(bench, bench.copy().scale(...))`, not
    `bench.animate.scale(...)`: a VGroup carries its own transparent rgba and
    animating one drags every child's opacity down. A copy has identical rgba
    at every node, so the interpolation is a pure move.
  * The deck's `_sticky_play` matches `FadeIn` OF a `RampTicker`, not the
    mobject alone. Matching the mobject also caught B4's clear-down, which
    fades the last ticker out, and opened a phantom second region.
